For the most accurate reproduction of the artwork, use a professional photo or fine-art printer and maintain the original A-series proportion of the file. Color and contrast may vary slightly depending on the printer, ink, paper and color-management settings used.

Satin or luster photographic paper is a practical choice for most print shops because it preserves color, contrast and detail while keeping reflections relatively controlled. Matte photographic paper may be used for a softer, lower-reflection finish, while glossy paper generally produces stronger reflections and a more saturated appearance. Fine-art baryta paper may also be used where available when deeper blacks and greater tonal depth are preferred.

Printed artwork should be kept away from prolonged direct sunlight, excessive humidity and significant temperature changes. Handle unframed prints by the edges whenever possible and keep the surface clean and dry. When framing, UV-filtering glass or acrylic can provide additional protection in brightly lit environments and help reduce long-term fading.
